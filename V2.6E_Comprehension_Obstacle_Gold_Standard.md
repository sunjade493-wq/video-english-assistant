# V2.6E Comprehension Obstacle Gold Standard

Purpose: define the authoritative explanation style for future comprehension obstacle generation.

Status: Gold Standard Frozen ✅

Scope: comprehension obstacle explanations only. This document extracts the style shared by the approved human-reviewed examples. It does not rewrite the approved examples and does not modify the 50-sample validation dataset.

## 1. Shared Structure

Every gold-standard comprehension obstacle explanation follows the same compact sequence:

```text
Expression:

[original subtitle expression]

字面意思：

[literal Chinese rendering]

实际意思：

[contextual Chinese meaning]

固定用法：

[prototype structure]

表示：

[core meaning of the prototype]
```

The structure has five required information blocks:

1. **Expression**
   - Shows the exact phrase or sentence fragment the learner met in the subtitle.
   - Keeps the learner anchored to the video moment.

2. **字面意思**
   - Gives a direct word-by-word Chinese reading.
   - Shows why the phrase may be confusing if interpreted literally.

3. **实际意思**
   - Gives the meaning the learner needs in the current conversational context.
   - Uses natural Chinese rather than abstract linguistic terminology.

4. **固定用法**
   - Shows the reusable prototype structure behind the expression.
   - Replaces accidental subtitle details with slots such as `somebody` or `something`.

5. **表示**
   - Gives the stable meaning of the prototype.
   - Helps the learner transfer the same pattern to future subtitle lines.

## 2. Why This Is Easier Than Dictionary-Style Explanation

Gold-standard explanations are easier because they are built for immediate comprehension, not for reference lookup.

Dictionary-style explanations often fail inside video learning because they tend to:

- start from English definitions instead of the learner's confusion;
- list too many possible meanings at once;
- use abstract labels such as idiom, phrasal verb, transitive verb, informal, or figurative;
- separate meaning from the exact subtitle expression;
- provide examples that are unrelated to the current video moment;
- require the learner to decide which dictionary sense applies.

The gold-standard style avoids those problems by following the learner's mental path:

```text
I saw this expression.
↓
The literal reading sounds strange.
↓
In this video, it actually means this.
↓
The reusable pattern is this.
↓
Next time I see the pattern, it means this type of thing.
```

This order reduces cognitive load. The learner does not need to compare dictionary senses or infer the relevant usage. The explanation makes the obstacle disappear quickly enough for the learner to return to the video.

## 3. Information Intentionally Included

The approved style intentionally includes only information that helps resolve the current comprehension obstacle.

### 3.1 Original Expression

Include the exact expression from the subtitle or the shortest meaningful fragment that caused the obstacle.

Purpose:

- confirms what is being explained;
- preserves the connection to the listening or subtitle moment;
- prevents the learner from feeling that the explanation is about a different phrase.

### 3.2 Literal Meaning

Include a simple Chinese literal meaning, even when it sounds awkward.

Purpose:

- exposes the source of misunderstanding;
- validates the learner's confusion;
- creates contrast with the actual meaning.

The literal meaning should be understandable, not polished.

### 3.3 Actual Meaning

Include the practical Chinese meaning needed to understand the line.

Purpose:

- answers the learner's real question: “这句话在这里到底什么意思？”
- keeps the explanation tied to comprehension rather than grammar study;
- lets the learner continue watching without stopping for research.

When the expression naturally supports more than one close reading in context, include multiple short Chinese lines.

### 3.4 Prototype Structure

Include a reusable structure under `固定用法`.

Purpose:

- teaches the pattern, not only the one subtitle instance;
- shows which parts are fixed and which parts can change;
- helps learners recognize future variations.

Preferred slot words:

- `somebody` for a person slot;
- `something` for a thing, task, message, responsibility, or situation slot;
- fixed words stay fixed exactly as the pattern requires.

### 3.5 Core Meaning of the Prototype

Include a short meaning under `表示`.

Purpose:

- states what the reusable structure generally means;
- supports transfer to future examples;
- avoids making the current subtitle variant the only thing learned.

## 4. Information Intentionally Excluded

The gold-standard style is intentionally narrow. It excludes information that may be correct but slows comprehension.

Do not include:

- dictionary-style sense numbering;
- etymology or historical origin;
- long grammar analysis;
- part-of-speech labels unless absolutely necessary;
- tense, aspect, voice, or clause analysis;
- register labels such as formal, informal, slang, unless required to avoid misuse;
- unrelated example sentences;
- pronunciation, phonetics, or word-level vocabulary notes;
- full bilingual sentence translation unless the whole sentence is the expression;
- warnings about rare meanings;
- exhaustive lists of all possible meanings;
- moral, cultural, or pragmatic commentary unless required for comprehension;
- generated alternative explanations that try to be more elegant than the approved style.

The explanation should not become a mini lesson. It should remove the obstacle and stop.

## 5. Appropriate Amount of Detail

The approved examples define a short-form standard.

### 5.1 Target Length

A complete explanation should usually contain:

- one expression;
- one literal meaning line;
- one to three actual meaning lines;
- one prototype structure;
- one to four short meaning lines under `表示`.

### 5.2 Sentence Style

Use short Chinese phrases rather than long explanatory paragraphs.

Preferred:

```text
不再让我参与这个项目
```

Avoid:

```text
这个短语在职场语境中通常表示某人因为某种原因被管理者从当前项目或任务中移除，不再承担相关职责。
```

The preferred version is faster, clearer, and better suited to video learning.

### 5.3 Explanation Depth

Include enough detail to distinguish literal meaning, contextual meaning, and reusable structure.

Do not include extra detail simply because it is linguistically accurate.

Gold-standard depth is:

```text
What did it literally sound like?
What does it actually mean here?
What fixed pattern should I remember?
What does that pattern mean?
```

Anything beyond those questions must earn its place by directly improving immediate comprehension.

## 6. Prototype Structure Rules

Prototype structures are central to the gold-standard style.

### 6.1 Use the Reusable Pattern, Not the One-Off Variant

The prototype should generalize the subtitle expression.

Examples of the principle:

```text
pull me off the project
→ pull somebody off something

lay it on us
→ lay something on somebody

give me a hand
→ give somebody a hand
```

The subtitle variant remains under `Expression`. The reusable pattern belongs under `固定用法`.

### 6.2 Preserve Fixed Words

Words that make the expression work should remain fixed in the prototype.

For example:

```text
call it a day
```

The whole phrase is fixed, so the prototype is also:

```text
call it a day
```

Do not force slots into a phrase if the approved pattern is fixed.

### 6.3 Use Slots Only Where Substitution Is Natural

Use `somebody` and `something` only for replaceable parts.

Good:

```text
get somebody
```

Good:

```text
give somebody a hand
```

Avoid over-generalizing into unnatural patterns.

### 6.4 Prototype Is Not a Grammar Lecture

The prototype is a recognition tool. It should not be followed by a long technical explanation.

The learner should be able to look at the prototype and immediately understand:

- what can change;
- what stays fixed;
- what the whole structure means.

## 7. Handling Multiple Meanings

Multiple meanings are allowed only when they are useful for understanding the expression.

### 7.1 Actual Meaning Can Contain Contextual Alternatives

If the expression can reasonably mean several close things in conversation, list them as separate short lines.

Example pattern:

```text
实际意思：

把消息告诉我们
把问题交给我们
让我们来承担
```

This works because the meanings are close and context-driven. They help the learner understand how the phrase functions.

### 7.2 Prototype Meaning Can Contain a Small Meaning Range

If the prototype naturally covers several related meanings, list them under `表示`.

Example pattern:

```text
表示：

抓到
说中
看穿
难住
```

This is acceptable because the expression is compact and each meaning is a common conversational reading of the same structure.

### 7.3 Do Not Add Exhaustive Dictionary Meanings

Multiple meanings should not become a dictionary entry.

Include only meanings that are:

- common;
- closely related to the expression;
- useful for video comprehension;
- short enough to scan instantly.

If a meaning is rare, technical, unrelated to the current subtitle, or likely to distract the learner, exclude it.

### 7.4 Keep Context First

The `实际意思` block should prioritize the current video meaning.

The `表示` block may broaden slightly to show the reusable pattern, but it should remain compact.

## 8. Why This Works Well Inside a Video Learning Workflow

Video learning is time-sensitive. The learner is watching, listening, pausing, replaying, and trying to keep emotional momentum. A comprehension obstacle explanation must therefore be fast, reassuring, and immediately actionable.

The gold-standard style works well because it:

- starts with the exact expression the learner just encountered;
- explains the mismatch between literal and actual meaning;
- uses Chinese for fast comprehension;
- avoids abstract grammar terms that interrupt viewing;
- gives a reusable prototype so the learner gains pattern recognition;
- keeps each block short enough to scan while paused;
- supports quick return to playback;
- focuses on removing the obstacle rather than teaching everything about the phrase;
- helps learners feel “I understand this line now,” which supports confidence and continuation.

In this workflow, the explanation is not a dictionary, a grammar lesson, or a writing assistant. It is a subtitle comprehension aid.

## 9. Generation Checklist

Before accepting a future comprehension obstacle explanation, verify:

- Does it keep the exact subtitle expression under `Expression`?
- Does it include a simple `字面意思`?
- Does it include the context-relevant `实际意思`?
- Does it include a reusable `固定用法` prototype?
- Does it include a short `表示` meaning for that prototype?
- Is the explanation short enough to scan during video learning?
- Does it avoid dictionary-style overload?
- Does it avoid unnecessary grammar terminology?
- Does it teach the pattern without rewriting the approved style?
- Are multiple meanings limited to short, useful, closely related meanings?
