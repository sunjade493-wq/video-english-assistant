# V2.6E Quality Validation Round 1

Purpose: human quality review dataset for comprehension obstacle explanations. This document contains 50 subtitle-style conversational English samples and does not change application behavior.

## 1. be up to something

Expression:

What are you up to?

Prototype Structure:

be up to something

Literal Meaning:

To be physically or mentally “up” toward an activity.

Actual Meaning:

To be doing something, planning something, or being involved in an activity. In conversation, “What are you up to?” often means “What are you doing?” or “What are you planning?”

Grammar Explanation:

`be up to + noun/gerund` treats the activity as the thing someone is currently connected with. `up to` can point to involvement, not physical height. That is why `What are you up to?` asks what activity or plan the person is involved in.

Common Extensions:

It can sound suspicious in `He's up to something`, meaning he may be secretly planning something. It can also mean ability or willingness in `I'm not up to going out tonight`.

Example Sentences:

1. You two are whispering again. What are you up to?
2. I was just up to some cleaning before you called.
3. She looks like she's up to something, and I don't like it.

## 2. get away with something

Expression:

Prototype Structure:

Literal Meaning:

To escape while carrying or having something.

Actual Meaning:

To do something wrong or risky without being punished or facing the expected consequences.

Grammar Explanation:

`get away` means escape. `with something` names the action or problem that should have caused trouble. Together, the structure means the person escapes the consequence that normally comes with that action.

Common Extensions:

It can apply to rule-breaking, bad behavior, weak work, or styling choices that somehow succeed despite being risky.

Example Sentences:

1. He lied to the boss and got away with it.
2. You can't get away with skipping every meeting.
3. Only she could get away with wearing sneakers to a wedding.

Prototype Structure:

Literal Meaning:

To possess an unspecified “thing” directed toward someone or something.

Actual Meaning:
